The Sound of Film: The Wondrous Impact of the Film Scores of Hans Zimmer

September 2023 · 9 min

Missing out on the opportunity to watch Hans Zimmer Live in one of the biggest arenas in Dubai would have been a tremendous tragedy. But I seized the opportunity the same way a starving lion would seize his share of meat: with ferocity. You may have picked up on the fact that I am a relatively spirited fan of Hans Zimmer. So what is it that I so fervently admire about him? To answer this, let's wind the clock back a few years…

Film scores: I wish I could say I had a clear progression regarding my budding interest in the subject, but I can't remember what it was that steered me toward this category of music instead of the contemporary rap songs I hear everyone listening to nowadays. I guess I can vaguely trace the origin of this interest in soundtrack music to the day I downloaded Spotify. Needless to say, the plethora of genres available, and scope to explore music on this app was extremely exciting. So eventually, after enough browsing around, I discovered the existence of the eminent music composer: Hans Zimmer.

Looking back, I wasn't very concerned with the composer himself (having no clue yet that my future interest in film would very strongly link back to him). What excited me was to see that one of his most played film scores was that of The Lion King. "The Lion King?!", I thought. "I love this soundtrack!" So, I browsed through various scores, each with unique names that effectively reflected the essence of the scene it was composed for. It is important to mention here that The Lion King is one of the childhood films that I watched and rewatched countless times only to appreciate the dialogue, story, and music tenfold. So, every time I clicked on one of the composed pieces by Zimmer titled things like, "I Was Just Trying to be Brave", or "Kings of the Past", I knew exactly what scene to envision from the movie. Among these, there is one specific composition that is very important to me: "Remember."

When I listen to this piece, I envision the culmination of the film. I find it to be a beautiful encapsulation of all the bits of music throughout the film, coming back full circle at the end to remind me of the tremendous journey that Simba (the protagonist) ventured through, and the emotional connections that he discovered, renewed, and revived. Remember - the name itself is a lesson; the music prompts me to always keep my family in mind the same way Simba remembers his, and to move on from the past, but to continue remembering the values you have learned from it. Along with providing an opportunity to reflect, this piece also provides me with a sense of calm. It is eclectic and soulful, rhythmic and extremely nostalgic. I especially enjoy listening to the end, when the music smoothly lifts and transitions into the iconic song "The Circle of Life", and crescendos as the finale.

There are times when I feel low, and somewhat overwhelmed by the several activities on my to-do list. But closing my eyes and humming "Remember" helps me meditate for a while. The music itself includes a harmonious choir that emanates a sense of unity. It has the potential to make me feel a little less lonely in the moment. The rhythm is a subtle explosion of colours and joy in my head that shifts my focus away from what's bothering me, allowing me to immerse myself in the film instead. It is during times like this that make me appreciate the composition of this music even more and realize the importance it has in my life – as a grounding force.

Listening to "Remember" was what prompted me to learn more about the genius behind its composition. My further research into Hans Zimmer and his other works led me to discover that he also composed for films like The Dark Knight and Pirates of the Caribbean. Learning that every film score I thoroughly enjoyed was composed by the same artist continually took me by surprise. A strike of serendipity to say the least; instead of having to attend multiple concerts to listen to these pieces played live, Hans Zimmer is the single artist with a composition of all my favorite film scores conveniently collected in a lovely bundle.

Coming back to the present, if you take a moment to reflect on your life, can you say that a certain event has successfully left you feeling mesmerised?

Having seen the Hans Zimmer Live show, I can. I entered the arena with little to no knowledge about how the event would run, so when it started with a phenomenal coordinated light show, a massive screen with beautiful accompanying visuals, and live stage performers for almost every piece, I was left with perpetual goosebumps. Particularly for the theme of The Lion King, an entire group of dancers made their way through the audience, swaying with the rhythmic flow of the music. Their costumes – drapes of vibrant, sunlit hues – seemed to radiate the warmth and energy carried by the dawning of a new day. Adorned with wooden, silver, and glass beads, the dancers embodied the beauty and richness of African culture. The enormous screen glowed bright orange with the visual of the rising sun… and I suddenly found myself transported to the savanna.

A few moments of applause later, the arena suddenly darkened, and an ominous drop of music rumbled through the audience; another brilliant score by Zimmer: "The Dark Knight." The upbeat, cheerful music of The Lion King is a stark contrast to this serious, suspenseful theme. It puts into perspective the effect such a film score can have on you, and its ability to completely alter the atmosphere. The Dark Knight film score for me is the epitome of adventure, thrill, and suspense. If the film is the roller coaster, its score is the thrilling drop of the ride.

An interview with Hans Zimmer, conducted by ABC News in 2012, gives us insight into the composer's masterful approach when crafting The Dark Knight's iconic score. "It's all about two notes," Zimmer explains, as he pushes down on the electric keyboard. A stark contrast of high and low notes slices the air, reverberating through the room. "That's a question, you see?" Then he shifts to a higher register, simply changing one note underneath the other, and we suddenly feel a sense of lightness beneath the tension. "And that's hope."

When composing the score for the film, he asked himself this question: "How do you describe the many facets of one character with just two notes?" It's a game of "musical chess" he's playing. The musical score of a film carries as much of the story as the characters do. Isn't it fascinating how story and music join hands in camaraderie to deliver to us what we call the wonder of cinema?

Another extremely exhilarating score played at the concert is from Pirates of the Caribbean. The swashbuckling theme is beautifully encapsulated in this piece, making me feel the thrill of clashing swords, and imagine myself escaping danger the way the infamous Jack Sparrow hilariously does. It is difficult to envision these adventurous scenes from films without their music soundtracks playing in the background. I think the effectiveness and power of a scene is amplified to the highest degree by its music because this allows us to feel the whole unencumbered cinematic experience that the world of film is meant to pull you into, one that I will always Remember.
