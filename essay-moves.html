Dealing With Moves

April 2024 · 8 min

Nobody moved.

Dad's face, a fortress of concentration, was illuminated behind his desktop screen. The only sign that indicated he was human and not just a human-looking statue was the rapid flickering of his eyes as he stared at the screen. He was so engrossed in his article on finance and the growing economy, I was sure that even a colossal wrecking ball forcefully swinging through the living room wouldn't deter him in the slightest. And given his enthusiasm in monopoly and finance, how fitting was it that the game I yearned to play was Monopoly Deal?

Unlike most teenage boys that are perpetually immersed within their virtual world – caught in a never-ending loop of game, sleep, eat, and repeat – my brother was studying intensely for his next test. With furrowed brows, deep in concentration, all he needed to complete his appearance as a wise scholar was a floor length robe and long grey beard. But if Dad wouldn't be deterred by the forceful swing of a wrecking ball bursting through the living room, my brother wouldn't even notice if that same wrecking ball was swinging straight at his head, so focused he was!

Meanwhile, Mom, an avid reader by day and a master of culinary arts by night, was orchestrating a symphony of flavours in the kitchen. She was preparing for dinner: swiftly chopping up the vegetables, tending to the pot of water until it reached a perfect simmer, artfully slicing the loaf of bread to go with the delicious savoury dish she'd worked on earlier in the day. The aroma of flavourful spices wandered toward us in the living room, and I detected the sharp smell of saffron infused in the air.

I notice all four of us breathe in a little deeper. From my spot on the carpet, I watch Mom's final slice of the knife, concluding her meal preparation. I suspect she needs a break before dinner time. In fact, I think all three of them do.

So, I gather up the splayed cards on the carpet from our last Monopoly Deal game, shuffle them not once, not twice, but a total of three times, because as my family always says: "You're so diligent in your work."

I deal out five cards to each of us and then wait for the others to move from their spots. I slide my leg over the carpet's softer areas beneath the sofa, sheltered from the friction of constant walking that's roughened most of it overtime. I listen to my dad's occasional click of the mouse as he scrolls down the article, my mother's soft humming as she glides across the kitchen, and my brother's delightful "Yesss" as he finally – after much perseverance and practice – answers the most challenging question correctly.

Someone will notice the set cards on the floor, and at least someone will move. But they're each too focused to do so. I dramatically clear my throat to get their attention, and gesture animatedly at the dealt cards as if to say, "I've been ready for a while now!" They each snap out of their casted spells, and I watch as Dad quickly scans the last paragraph of his article, my brother answers the last part of the question, and Mom puts away the last few dishes, before they all meet me on the floor.

We're ruthless when we slide into game-mode: stealthily stealing the ideal property card to complete our set, asking for monstrous amounts of rent condemning each of us to perpetually teeter on the verge of bankruptcy, and finally, ganging up on the unfortunate player – usually my poor brother – by collecting terrible debts and full property sets from him and him alone. And once we start the game, we're all itching for our turn to make that devastating move.

I haven't played Monopoly Deal with my family in a while because this time, someone did move; it happened to be me. I moved away from my routine-driven, hardworking, tightly knit family and into my new living room, bedroom, and study room which all happen to be one single room: my dorm room. But even in a new country, a new setting, a new routine, I haven't stopped moving. Constantly! From class to class, and from friend to friend… it's impossible to stay still.

Our family calls don't last very long sometimes, but they happen every single day. Moving away from home is as new of an experience for me as it is for my family. We're forced to adjust to a gap in the group, adapt to a new lifestyle, and ask questions like: who's the game planner now? Who sets up the cards? Who calls everybody to the floor when it's time to play? The answer to those questions is this: it's any one of us.

Like water seamlessly moves into its mould, my family has taken the shape of their new frame. Mom glides onto the floor and studies her dealt cards, then proceeds to theatrically complain about her garbage luck this round. My brother – who miraculously escaped the role of victim and targeted rent collection (but look out, Dad, because you're the next victim) – suspiciously smiles, holding onto his cards like they are ammunition waiting to be fired. But even as I watch them play from my spot on the sofa ten thousand kilometres away through the tiny screen on my phone, we all know he's safeguarding the Deal Breaker, because his poker face is as good as tuna fish is on chocolate ice cream. It's not.

Now I deal the cards again. But this time, to three of my floormates sitting together in our common room. They're lounging on the sofas, and we've kept a saffron scented candle burning by the window, while I shuffle the cards. The sweet and earthy fragrance permeates the room and for a moment, I find myself dealing the cards out to my brother, mom, and dad. I shuffle not once, not twice, but three times, and look up to see my friends watching me, not my family. From her cross-legged position on the sofa with her hand under her chin, my friend comments on my shuffling: "You're very diligent in your work." And in that moment, I truly was moved.
